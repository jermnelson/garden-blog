# Hail and Thunderstorms
Today after I got back from my weekly shopping trip to Durango,
the weather started first as rain, following by heavier rain accompanied 
by thunder and lightening. Next it shifted to dime and nickel size
hail and here is a photo after the last on the path leading to the garden:

![Hail on Path](img/02026-06-26-hail-on-path.png)

Here is the Southwest Garden Bed with the second tomato on the left most
damaged. The two bean plans were more damaged from the hail. The white 
dots are about 3 or 4 millimeter in diameter. 

In the Northwest bed of potatoes and onions, the middle potato row survived
pretty well, you can see somewhat large hail stones on the left.

![Northwest bed, middle potato row hail](img/02026-06-26-northwest-middle-potato-hail.png)

You can see more hail in this photo of the North edge row of potatoes:

![Northwest bed, edge potato row hail](img/02026-06-26-northwest-bed-potato-edge-hail.png)

More challenges as a high-altitude farmer in Bayfield, Colorado. Our region 
definitely needs the moisture to help suppress the wild fires in our region.

My brother Jarrett is a firefighter and I am sure he is on some line now in 
extinguishing one of these fires. Holding him in the light.
